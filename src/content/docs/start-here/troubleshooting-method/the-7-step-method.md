## In one sentence

A fixed order for working any problem, so you move from symptom to fix by
evidence rather than by guessing.

## How it works

The classic structured method has seven steps. Work them in order; do not skip to
step 5.

1. **Identify the problem.** Gather symptoms, ask the user, and reproduce it if
   you can. Note what changed recently.
2. **Establish a theory of probable cause.** Start with the simplest likely
   explanation. Question the obvious first.
3. **Test the theory.** Confirm or rule it out. If it is wrong, form a new theory
   or escalate.
4. **Make a plan of action.** Decide the fix and consider the side effects before
   you touch anything.
5. **Carry out the plan.** Apply the fix, one change at a time, so you know what
   worked.
6. **Verify full functionality.** Confirm the fix with the user, and take
   preventive steps if you can.
7. **Document everything.** Record the cause, the fix, and the outcome in the
   ticket.

:::tip
Change one thing at a time. If you change three settings at once and it works,
you have not learned which one was the fix.
:::

:::caution
Before any change that could lose data, confirm there is a backup or the user
accepts the risk. Step 4 exists so you think about side effects first.
:::

## Why it matters on the helpdesk

Under pressure it is tempting to jump straight to a fix you half-remember. The
method keeps you honest: it stops you applying random fixes, makes your tickets
reproducible, and gives you something clear to hand over if you escalate.
