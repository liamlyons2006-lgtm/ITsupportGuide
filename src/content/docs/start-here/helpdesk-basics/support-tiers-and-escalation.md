## In one sentence

Support is layered into tiers by depth of knowledge, and escalation is handing a
ticket up a tier with enough detail that the next person does not start over.

## How it works

The tiers describe who normally handles what, not how important a person is:

- **L1 (first line)** — the front door. Password resets, account unlocks, known
  fixes, and triage. Resolves the common cases and routes the rest.
- **L2 (second line)** — deeper technical work: server, network, and endpoint
  issues that need more access or specialist knowledge.
- **L3 (third line)** — the deepest experts, often the people who own a system or
  the vendor behind it. Code-level bugs, architecture, and root-cause analysis.

**Escalation** is moving a ticket up a tier when it is outside your remit or you
are stuck. A good escalation includes:

1. What the user reported, in their words.
2. What you have already tried, and the result of each step.
3. The exact error text, event IDs, or screenshots.
4. The impact and priority, and who to contact.

:::caution[Escalate]
Escalate instead of guessing when a fix needs access you do not have, could cause
data loss, or touches security. In the ticket, include the four points above so
L2 can continue without re-asking the user.
:::

## Why it matters on the helpdesk

Clean escalation is a big part of how an L1 tech is judged. Escalating too early
wastes L2's time; escalating too late frustrates the user. The skill is knowing
your boundary and handing over a ticket that is ready to work.
